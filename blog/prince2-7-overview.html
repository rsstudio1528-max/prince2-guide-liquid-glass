PRINCE2 7 overview

A compact way to picture the current structure: principles guide behavior, people stay central, practices describe ongoing management work, and processes describe the project journey.

Reference pagePRINCE2 7Use the four-part structure as a memory aid, not a substitute for the official manual.

The four-part study map

Principles

Enduring guidance for keeping the project management approach coherent.

People

Behavior, relationships, communication, leadership, and the human side of project work.

Practices

Recurring management areas such as business case, organizing, plans, quality, risk, issues, and progress.

Processes

A structured project journey, from starting through directing, managing stages, and closing.

Management products

Projects need information that can support decisions. Management products are the records, plans, reports, and other information used to understand the state of the project and guide action. Their detail and format can be tailored to the project.

Memory cue: “Why · Who · How · When”Principles explain how to stay aligned with the method. People explains who is involved and how they interact. Practices cover recurring management needs. Processes show when key management actions occur.

Roles are part of the control system

PRINCE2 makes accountability visible. The project board provides direction, the project manager manages the project within agreed boundaries, and team-level responsibilities connect specialist delivery back to the project's management controls. The exact organizational naming can vary when tailored.

Management by stages

Instead of treating the project as one uninterrupted block, the method uses stages as useful review points. At a stage boundary, decision-makers can examine progress, updated forecasts, risks, issues, and the continuing justification before agreeing what happens next.

How to study the structure

  1. Learn the four study buckets.
  2. Attach a concrete project example to each bucket.
  3. Then learn the terms and management products used inside them.
