Make the method practical

PRINCE2 project management

The method becomes useful when it shapes everyday choices: what to plan, what to review, what to escalate, and what to record.

Practical guideControl rhythmUse the checklist below as a lightweight conversation starter with a project team.

1. Start with justification

Make the reason for the project visible. What outcome is expected? What changes if the project succeeds? What assumptions matter? A useful business case is not a ceremonial document; it provides the reference point for continued decisions.

2. Plan at the level you can control

Plan work so that ownership, timing, dependencies, quality expectations, and acceptance are clear enough to manage. Avoid inventing detail that will become obsolete before the team can use it.

3. Manage by stages

Use stages to create a manageable review rhythm. At the end of a stage, compare actual performance with what was expected, refresh the forecasts, look at risks and issues, and decide whether the next stage should proceed.

4. Work inside tolerances

Tolerances define the boundaries within which the project manager can act without asking for a higher-level decision. When an exception threatens those boundaries, the matter can be escalated using the agreed governance path.

Practical testIf a control does not help a decision-maker understand where the project is, what has changed, or what decision is needed, ask whether the control can be simplified or tailored.

5. Treat issues and risks differently

Risk

An uncertain event or condition that could affect objectives. The project chooses how to respond before the uncertainty becomes reality.

Issue

A problem, concern, request, or other matter that already exists and needs a decision or action.

6. Define “done” before delivery

Quality expectations make acceptance visible. The team should understand what a product needs to be like, how it will be checked, and who has authority to accept it.

7. Close deliberately

Closing is more than stopping the schedule. Confirm products have been accepted, capture lessons, review remaining actions, and make the transition or handover explicit so that the project's benefits can continue beyond the project team.
