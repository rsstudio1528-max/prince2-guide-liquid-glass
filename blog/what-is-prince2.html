What is PRINCE2?

PRINCE2 is less about telling specialists how to perform their craft and more about creating a management system around the project.

6 min readMental modelFocus on the relationships between justification, control, roles, and delivery.

Imagine a project as a small organization with a temporary mission. It needs a reason to exist, a way to make decisions, people who understand their responsibilities, and a rhythm for reviewing whether the work is still worth doing. PRINCE2 organizes those needs into a coherent management approach.

What the framework covers

Direction

Project-level decisions are made through defined accountability instead of informal escalation.

Planning

Plans describe what should be delivered, when, with what resources, and under which controls.

Quality

Products have clear expectations and acceptance thinking, rather than “done” being a vague feeling.

Risk & change

Uncertainty and requested changes are managed through explicit decisions and records.

What it is not

PRINCE2 is not a replacement for subject-matter expertise, engineering practice, design methods, agile delivery techniques, or an organization's own technical standards. A project can use specialist delivery approaches while using PRINCE2 for governance and management.

A project is a feedback loop

At the start, the project has assumptions and an intended outcome. As work progresses, new information appears. Good project management creates moments to review that information, compare it with the project's justification and tolerances, and decide what to do next.

A useful picturePRINCE2 provides the guardrails. The project team chooses the driving technique that fits the work, while governance keeps the journey aligned with the reason the project exists.

Tailor the management system

Tailoring is central to making a method usable. The controls for a small internal improvement should not look identical to those for a long, multi-supplier programme. Start with the management needs, then choose the level of formality that gives decision-makers the information they need without creating unnecessary overhead.
