PRINCE2 key terms

A practical glossary for the words that show up most often while learning PRINCE2. The descriptions below are deliberately plain-language study notes.

GlossaryStudy aidUse the official guidance for formal definitions and exam preparation.
Business case

The management view of why the project is worthwhile, including the expected value, cost, risk, and continuing justification.

Project board

The project-level direction and decision-making body that represents key interests and provides governance.

Project manager

The role responsible for day-to-day management of the project within agreed boundaries and governance.

Project product

The overall outcome the project is expected to deliver.

Stage

A management section of the project used to plan and control work at a useful level of detail.

Exception

A situation where forecast performance may move beyond an agreed tolerance and requires a higher-level decision.

Tolerance

The permitted range for an objective such as time, cost, scope, quality, risk, or benefit before escalation is required.

Work package

A defined slice of work handed to a team or delivery role with clear expectations and controls.

Lesson

Knowledge captured from experience so future decisions and delivery approaches can improve.

Management product

Information created and maintained to support project management, control, communication, or decision-making.

A memory method

When you meet a new term, ask three questions: “Who uses it?”, “What decision does it support?”, and “At what point in the project does it matter?” A word becomes easier to remember when it is attached to a real management situation.

Example“Tolerance” becomes concrete when you picture a project manager saying: “We can finish within this agreed time range without escalating. Outside that boundary, the project board needs to decide.”
